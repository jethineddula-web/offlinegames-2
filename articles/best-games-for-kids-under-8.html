Best games for kids under 8: what we recommend and why

Not every game in the vault is for a 6-year-old. The vault holds a range of cartridges. Some are Teen for stylised combat. Some need reading. Some are hard on purpose. For under-8s, we recommend eight Everyone-rated games that work with a thumb, have no chat, no store, and no scary fail state.

This is not an official rating — we apply our own labels conservatively. If a game is on a boundary, we put it in the higher tier. That is explained in choosing games for young children and in the 9 Sep News update where we added ratings to every cover.

The 8 we hand over

  • Crayon Kingdom (Everyone) — coloring, no timer, no fail. Tap a region to fill. 40KB, instant. Best for 3-5 year olds. No ads inside game.
  • Fruit Trio (Everyone) — match-3 with orchard colors, short boards. Chain cascade is satisfying, not punishing. No chat, no board ads.
  • Puzzle Break (Everyone) — jigsaw-style, remember pieces, rebuild picture. Slow, intentional, good for co-play parent+child.
  • Magic Jars (Everyone) — pour-and-sort liquids until each jar is clean. Rewards patience, two pours ahead planning. Good cooldown.
  • Tic-Tac-Toe (Everyone) — classic 3×3. Teach center-first, then opposite corners. See never-lose guide. 60-second matches.
  • Words Matching (Everyone) — word wheel + crossword tiles, dictionary bundled offline. For 6-8 year olds who read. See teacher guide classroom ideas.
  • Numberly (Everyone) — connect numbers 1-2-3 across grid, serene palette. No timer, undo tool. New September puzzle, built for calm.
  • Save The Dog (Everyone) — draw lines to save pup from bees, physics lines, quick levels. Family-friendly, 1-minute stages.

Total size for all eight: under 2MB. Cache once on WiFi, then airplane mode works. See what offline means for service worker details.

What we exclude and why

Teen-rated Dead Streets (zombie shooter) and Umbra Fight (brawler) are not in this list. They are labeled Teen on cover and on play page, with note: contains stylised combat. Not intended for young children. Even Everyone 10+ titles like Balance Ball and Impossible Master are left out for under-8s because difficulty curve assumes reading and persistence.

We also avoid games with time pressure for this age. Pop-a-Lock is Everyone but 30-second timing is stressful for 4-year-olds. Save it for 8+.

For parents handing a phone over, that matters: you want no chat with strangers, no loot boxes, no push notifications, no account wall. All our games meet that — no hidden chat, no energy system. Ads are on page shell, not inside game canvas, so when offline, game still runs without ads.

How to test offline before a flight

Open vault on hotel WiFi, open each of the eight games for 5 seconds, close overlay. Service worker og-v8 caches them. Then enable airplane mode, refresh page, tap cover. If it loads, you are good. High scores and coloring progress save in localStorage, not cache, so they survive cache clear but not browser data clear.

On school Chromebooks, many schools block external game domains but allow same-domain. Because our games live under offlinegames.art/games/ same domain, they work where iframe portals don't. For IT, whitelist offlinegames.art and it covers all games. See teacher guide for pre-cache workflow.

FAQs for parents

Does it collect data? No account, no login. LocalStorage for high scores only, stays in your browser. See Privacy — Advertising and cookies section explains AdSense with opt-out links.

Is there chat? No. No multiplayer chat, no external links inside game. Contact page is outside games.

Can my child buy something? No. No store, no in-app purchases, no loot boxes. Price is 0, stated in VideoGame schema on each play page.

How do I know rating? Every cover in grid shows badge: Everyone, Everyone 10+, Teen. Play page repeats it with note. We apply conservatively — boundary goes higher.

Setting up a kid-safe vault at home

Create a bookmark folder called “Kids Vault” with only the eight Everyone links above. On iPad, add each to Home Screen — they open standalone, no address bar, no way to wander to Teen titles. On Android, use Digital Wellbeing to limit to 30 minutes. Test airplane mode once: if game loads offline, it will work in car without data. For full privacy notes, see Privacy and parent guide.
