Browser games for teachers: classroom ideas that actually work

Browser games in a classroom need three things: same-domain hosting so school filters allow them, offline cache so they survive flaky Wi-Fi, and no chat or store so you can hand a Chromebook over without worrying. The vault was built for that. This guide gives concrete lesson hooks for September's new cartridges plus the older ones teachers already use.

I'm Jethin, I built offlinegames.art as a solo dev. No accounts, no login, no newsletter. Games live under /games/ on same domain, not third-party iframes. That matters for IT whitelisting — you whitelist one domain, you get every game. For privacy, see Privacy — high scores in localStorage only, AdSense disclosed with opt-out.

Math: Math Runner, Numberly, 2048

Math Runner (Everyone, new Sep 7) — 3D runner where you hit gates: ×2, +8, ÷3, then battle monster with your number. Lesson hook: 10-minute warm-up on multiplication vs division trade-offs. Ask: “Gate says ÷3 but next says ×4, net?” Students run, then calculate net on board. Works offline after first load, 1.2MB.

Numberly (Everyone, Sep 11) — connect numbers 1-2-3 in order without crossing lines. Teaches sequential reasoning, path planning. For 2nd grade: easy levels, for 6th: advanced with obstacles. No timer, undo tool, serene palette reduces anxiety. See best for kids under 8.

2048 — powers of two, exponential growth. Corner rule is planning. Use our 2048 strategy guide to show snake pattern. 5-minute challenge: who reaches 512 with fewest moves?

Vocabulary and spelling: Words Matching, Word Magic

Words Matching (Sep 11) — word wheel + crossword tiles, dictionary bundled offline. Students swipe wheel to find hidden words, fill tiles. No server fetch mid-game, so works in airplane mode. For ELL: set 5-minute timer, count words found. For native: bonus words for extra gems.

Word Magic — build spells from letters, clear grid. Similar offline dictionary. Good for spelling patterns -tion, -ing. Pair with paper: students write found words, define one.

Both link to short sessions guide for 2-minute vocabulary bursts between lessons.

Logic and spatial: Arrows Puzzle, Bees Escape, Sudoku

Arrows Puzzle (Sep 7) — minimal arrow maze, follow arrows to exit. Teaches spatial logic, two turns ahead. 40KB, instant restart. For 1st grade: physical version with floor arrows, then digital.

Bees Escape (Sep 7) — guide swarm through arrows and blocks, pathfinding. Short stages, ideal for 2-minute brain breaks. No fail shame — swarm just retries.

Sudoku — scanning, pencil marks, hidden singles, naked pairs. See Sudoku 4 techniques. Easy grid is 6-10 minutes, good for early finishers. No timer, so no pressure.

Classroom logistics that actually work

Whitelisting: IT needs one entry: offlinegames.art. All games same domain, no external game CDN. Fonts and AdSense are async and non-blocking — if school blocks AdSense, games still run. We tested on school Chromebook with uBlock.

Pre-cache for no-signal rooms: Open vault once on strong WiFi, open each game you want for 5 seconds, close overlay. Our service worker caches with cache:no-cache revalidation (see how we built vault offline-first). Next day in room with weak WiFi, games load from cache instantly. For 30-student cart, do this once per device cart, not per student.

No-download argument: No app store, no install, no 200MB Unity. Pop-a-Lock 40KB, 2048 80KB. See why no-download matters for storage math — shared tablets fill fast.

Ratings: Every cover shows Everyone / Everyone 10+ / Teen. For under-8s, use only Everyone set from best for kids under 8. Teen titles Dead Streets and Umbra Fight are excluded from classroom lists.

5 ready lesson plans (10 min each)

1. Math Runner ×4: Play 3 minutes, then board: “You had 12, hit ×2 then ÷3, what now? 8.” Discuss order.

2. Words Matching vocab: 5 minutes play, then each student writes 3 found words + sentence.

3. Arrows Puzzle floor: Tape arrows on floor, students walk, then digital version.

4. Numberly paths: Show one grid, ask to plan path without crossing, then play.

5. Chess openings: 10-minute openings guide — control centre, develop, castle by 10.
