Choosing browser games for young children: a parent's guide to the vault

OfflineGames is family-friendly, but family-friendly doesn't mean every game is for a six-year-old. We have colouring and word puzzles next to a zombie shooter and a hard arcade run. To avoid a child tapping the wrong cover, every cartridge has a content rating you can see before playing.

Our three ratings — and what they actually mean

Everyone — No violence, no scary imagery, no time pressure that punishes. Suitable to hand to a young child unsupervised. Examples: Crayon Kingdom (colouring), Sudoku (logic), Words Matching, Tic-Tac-Toe, Fruit Trio.

Everyone 10+ — Cartoon peril or difficulty that assumes reading and persistence. Nothing that would upset a child, but some would frustrate a very young one. Examples: 2048 (needs strategy), Balance Ball (physics, fails often), Pop-a-Lock (timing pressure).

Teen — Stylised combat, dark atmosphere, or themes not intended for young children. Currently two games: Dead Streets (zombie shooter, dark streets, simple guns) and Umbra Fight (stick fighting). Both have no blood, no gore, but tension and combat.

These are our own labels, not an official board like ESRB or PEGI, and we apply them conservatively: anything on a boundary goes in higher tier.

What we collect and what we don't — for parents

We don't require account, we don't collect name, email, or location unless you email us via Contact. High scores and favourites stay in browser's localStorage — they never reach our server. See Privacy Policy for full text.

Advertising: We use Google AdSense. Google uses cookies to serve ads based on prior visits. You can opt out via Google Ads Settings. For children, personalised ads are restricted in EEA/UK — they see non-personalised ads after consent message.

We do not have chat, multiplayer with strangers, or user-generated content. No child can talk to a stranger through our games. All games are single-player or hot-seat (same device).

Age-by-age suggestions

4-6 years: Crayon Kingdom (colouring, no fail), Fruit Trio (match-three, slow), Puzzle Break (jigsaw, no timer). Play together first 5 minutes to show tap and drag.

7-9 years: Sudoku Easy, Tic-Tac-Toe, Words Matching, Arrows Puzzle, Numberly. These teach logic without time pressure. Let them use pencil marks in Sudoku — it's learning, not cheating.

10-12 years: 2048, Balance Ball, Pop-a-Lock, Magic Jars, Math Runner. These need planning and patience. Good for short breaks, not long sessions.

13+: Chess 3D, Apex Drift, Dead Streets (if you are okay with stylised combat), Impossible Master. Chess especially benefits from our openings guide.

How to set up a kid-safe session

  1. Open vault, tap category button, choose Puzzle or Board — filters out Shooting/Teen automatically.
  2. Use search: type "Everyone" — shows only Everyone-rated games (we index rating in search).
  3. On phone, add to Home Screen — opens in standalone mode, no address bar, less chance to navigate away.
  4. Play first game together to show how to close overlay with ✕ — so child doesn't get stuck.

If you think a rating is wrong, tell us. We would rather re-rate than defend a borderline call. Recent example: we moved Sky Dodge from Everyone to Everyone 10+ because crash animation was tense for 5-year-olds.

Screen time and offline

All games work offline after first load (see what offline means). That means you can load a few Everyone games on WiFi, then go airplane mode and still play — no ads load offline, no new content can be fetched, which some parents prefer for focused time.

We don't have daily rewards, loot boxes, or push notifications. No dark patterns to keep playing. Close tab, game unloads, next cartridge starts fresh.

How we test ratings

We play each game for 20 minutes and note: is there sudden loud sound? Is there a fail animation that could scare a 4-year-old? Is there text that requires reading? If yes to any, rating goes up. Example: Balance Ball has no violence but ball falls with a thud and level restarts instantly — that instant fail frustrates 5-year-olds, so Everyone 10+.

We also watch kids play. Our test group is 6-year-old and 9-year-old of a team member. If 6-year-old quits in 2 minutes saying boring, it's not Everyone — it's 10+. If 9-year-old asks what a word means and can't proceed, we add clearer instructions or raise rating.

Ratings are conservative by design. We would rather a 12-year-old be bored by an Everyone game than a 5-year-old scared by a Teen game.

Building a routine

Instead of unlimited screen time, try 15-minute vault sessions: 5 minutes puzzle (Sudoku), 5 minutes creative (Crayon Kingdom), 5 minutes active (Sky Dodge). Our games have no daily streaks, so no pressure to come back tomorrow. Close tab, done.

For classrooms, teachers have used Words Matching for vocabulary and Math Runner for arithmetic — both Everyone-rated, no ads inside game, work offline after first load, so they work on school Chromebooks without extra permissions.
